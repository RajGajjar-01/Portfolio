## The problem

Twelve services published events to Kafka and about thirty consumed them.
Nothing validated that a producer's change was safe for its consumers — so
roughly once a month someone renamed a field and broke a downstream pipeline,
usually discovered hours later in a dashboard that had quietly gone flat.

## Approach

The fix had to fail *before* the deploy, not after, or it would just be
another alert to ignore.

- Every event type gets a versioned schema stored in the registry.
- A CI check diffs the proposed schema against the current one and classifies
  the change as backward compatible, forward compatible, or breaking.
- Breaking changes fail the build unless the author explicitly registers a new
  major version and declares a migration window.

## Outcome

Consumer-breaking incidents went from roughly one a month to zero across the
following two quarters. The unexpected benefit was documentation: the registry
became the place people looked to understand what events existed at all.

## What I would change

Compatibility classification is conservative — it flags some changes as
breaking that are provably safe. Engineers learned to work around it with a
manual override, which is exactly the escape hatch you do not want people
reaching for by habit.
